Is there still good business here?

Yes — but not the business you were running in 2022. Three facts settle it. Your conversion got better while volume fell, so the machine works. Your fees went up (€14.3k → €16.4k perm), so the market still pays. And contract delivery runs at a 38-day median fill, which is fast. What you have is not a broken business — it is a working business with the top of its funnel switched off and a contractor book quietly leaking. Both are fixable, and one of them is nearly free.

The single most important number for 2026

The contractor book is shrinking by about five heads a month.

Contract is the half of the business that did not cross the March separation, so it is the clean read — and it is draining. Over Oct–May you finished 53 contractors a month and replaced only 48.4 of them. That gap, not new business, is why the weekly run-rate has fallen from €160,753 to €112,877.

Where the book goes each month

Oct 2025 – May 2026, per month. Replacement is running below attrition.
What it costs. Each contractor is worth about €573 a week. Drifting −4.6 a month burns €136,000 of annualised run-rate every month it continues. Left alone for a year that is roughly €1.6M of run-rate gone — and it compounds, because a smaller book generates fewer extensions.
And what fixes it. 208 contractors finished with no extension, CV, interview or placement activity logged at all — 56% of everyone who finished. Converting just one in five of those into an extension adds 42 heads and turns the book positive (+1.4/month). One in three turns it to +5.2/month. This needs no new clients, no new candidates, and no new BD — only a call before the assignment ends.

One honest caveat. The €112,877 is measured now, at the end of the weak summer season, against an Oct–May average that covers the strong months. Some of the −30% is seasonal. The net −4.6/month is not — that is computed inside the Oct–May window itself, and it is the number to act on.

Targets to set — built from your ratios, not from ambition

Your old targets assumed conversions 1.5–5.8× better than you have ever achieved.

The 2025 plan asked for a 35% call→meeting rate against an actual 10.7%, and for 38% of jobs to be A-grade against an actual 6.7%. Every one of these is back-solved from what Gentis actually converts at. Set them at the commit column; resource against plan.

Anchor the whole system on two numbers. Extension rate (currently 69% of finishers — target 80%) and CVs sent per head per day (currently 1.24 — target 1.7). Everything else on this page follows from those two. Do not set a daily call target: the call→A-job ratio has not moved in four years (88:1 then, 88:1 now), so calls buy jobs at a fixed and expensive rate.

Freelance desks, ranked

By weekly GP per contractor — the only unit that matters when revenue accrues weekly.

Contract economics are a rate, not a fee: a contractor out at €838 a week earns €38.5k a year, one at €488 earns €22.4k. Two desks stand out for opposite reasons — Luxembourg IT has the highest weekly rate, Brussels IT Infrastructure the highest margin at 27.2%. Your largest desk, Brussels IT Dev, is the worst on both.

The margin drag has names. Your book median margin is 15.6%. Five accounts with three or more placements sit below it — UCB at 11.0%, Electrabel at 11.9%, Belfius at 12.1%, AG Insurance at 12.6%. They carry 21% of sampled GP. Repricing just those to the book median is worth roughly €400,000 a year. Repricing the whole book back to its 2022–25 level of 19.4% is worth about €1.5M a year — more than any activity lever, and it requires zero extra placements.

What to build into the CRM — in this order

Sized against what one team can realistically ship.

You asked what your current capacity can cover. The honest answer: not all of it, and you should not try. Cube19 is a reporting layer over a data model. Rebuilding the reporting is weeks of work; rebuilding the data model that makes reporting possible is the part that cannot be retrofitted. Build in the order below and you can stop at any line and still have something that works.

The one thing you must not get wrong. Record a timestamped row every time a submission enters a stage — not the submission's current status. Every ratio on every page I have built for you comes from stage-entry events. A CRM that stores only "this candidate is at interview" can never reproduce any of it, and you will not discover that until you try, a year from now, with no Cube19 left to check against.

What the system can predict — and how well

The limit is not the model, it is the logging. Forecasts inherit the quality of the inputs. Right now 36% of contract placements have a placeholder owner, 56% of finishers have no logged activity, and 85% of new candidates are never sent anywhere. Fix those three and the same engine goes from directional to dependable — because the maths was never the hard part.
