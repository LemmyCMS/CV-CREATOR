The short answer

Two half-time recruiters is one full-time desk. Starting cold in September, at Gentis's measured conversion rates and with the CRM doing what it is built to do, expect around 10 placements and roughly €170,000 billed in year one — with a realistic range of €100k to €277k depending on how fast the sourcing works and how well you convert cold relationships. That is a real desk, not a hobby, and the timing is right: September starts Gentis's strong season, which runs through May.

The number that matters more than the billing. If you weight toward freelance you bill slightly less in year one but end it with 10 contractors out and about €328,000 a year already running before you place anything in year two. Perm pays now; freelance pays every year after. See the next section — this is the single biggest strategic choice you make.

Perm banks. Freelance compounds.

The one structural lesson in Gentis's whole dataset.

Gentis's €8.18M of contract GP is not 197 clever placements — it is 197 contractors out, each generating about €713 a week for as long as they stay. A perm fee of €17,382 is earned once. A contractor at €713/week is worth €32,800 a year, every year, for doing nothing further. On the same placement count, the difference after three years is not marginal.

The same 10 placements, three years out

Cumulative GP, placing at the same rate each year. Contractors stay ~12 months, so the freelance book fills in year one and then holds — it does not stack forever.
The catch, and Gentis proved it. A freelance book only compounds if contractors get extended. Gentis extends 69% of finishers, and 56% of their finishers had no extension conversation logged at all — so their book drains by 4.6 heads a month and their run-rate has fallen from €160,753 to €112,877 a week. Build end-date alerting into the CRM before you place your first contractor, not after.

The first two months produce almost nothing, and that is correct rather than pessimistic — you cannot send CVs to jobs you have not won. Months one and two are pure business development, and the plan scores them that way.

What a steady week looks like, per recruiter

Once the desk is running, from March.
On the call target. Gentis logs 3.6 client calls per head per day. Do not copy that — it is a mature desk with an existing client base, and it is almost certainly under-logged. A startup desk at 15–20 calls a day is not aggressive, it is simply what winning a client base from zero costs. It is also the only input entirely within your control, which makes it the right thing to hold people to in months one and two when there is no output yet.

What the CRM is actually worth

Each uplift traced to a leak measured in Gentis's data.

The honest framing: the CRM buys you throughput and retention, not conversion. Whether a client likes your candidate is a market and relationship question that no software changes. How many candidates you can put in front of them, and whether you notice a contract ending, are pure process — and that is where all the value is.

The sourcing bugs are the critical path. The entire difference between the low case (€103k) and the base case (€174k) is CVs per day — 1.6 against 2.2. That is the sourcing and matching pipeline working. Nothing else on your build list moves year-one revenue by 70%. Fix sourcing before you build a single dashboard.

What I cannot tell you

Conversion rates, fee sizes, contractor economics and seasonality are computed from Gentis's Cube19 output, Sep 2022 – Sep 2026. Assumed for The Zig: a 12-month new-desk ramp, 2.5 working days per week per half-time recruiter, 12-month average assignment tenure, and cold-relationship conversion at 65–100% of Gentis's measured rate. Desk rankings rest on single-page samples — 65 perm and 80 contract placements — and are a starting hypothesis, not a verdict.

Freelance GP assumes a 19.4% margin, the level Gentis held in 2022–25 before drifting to 15.6%. Holding that margin is a validation rule in the CRM, not a negotiation skill.
