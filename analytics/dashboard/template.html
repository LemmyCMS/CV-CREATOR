Year by year

Complete years only.

GP per head is on the toggle for a reason: a rising GP line and a rising headcount line look identical on a revenue chart, and confusing the two drives more bad strategy than any other mistake in this business. Partial years at the edge of the data are excluded — one would read as a collapse.

Ramp curve

Target new starters against this curve, not the desk average. Targeting them at desk average guarantees failure and early attrition.

Target back-solver

Set the target at p80. Plan capacity at p50.

Walk the ratio chain backwards from a revenue goal to the activity that produces it. Each ratio is uncertain, and eight uncertain numbers multiply — which is why the band widens as you go up the funnel. Targeting the median means missing half the time by construction, so the commitment goes at p80 and the resourcing plan at p50.

Where the money actually is

Elasticity is flat across a multiplicative chain — a 10% gain is worth the same wherever you get it. What separates these is headroom: how far each can realistically move.

Proportions are Beta-Binomial posteriors with a 90% credible interval; counts-per-count use a Gamma-Poisson posterior; money-per-effort uses a bootstrap over the actual fee distribution, because fees are skewed enough that a normal assumption would badly understate the spread. Markets are compared against a leave-one-out peer baseline. Trend and momentum use complete years only.

Benchmark notches are industry reference points, not this firm's history. They exist so the system produces sane output before enough data exists to replace them — and every one of them should be replaced by a measured value.
